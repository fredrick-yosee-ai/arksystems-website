## Section 2 — The problem

**No call to action here.**

**H2**
> AI spending fails when it starts with the technology instead of the business case.

**Body**
> The tools aren't the constraint. The constraint is that most businesses can't identify where AI produces a return in their own operation, what that return is worth, or what capturing it would cost. Without those three figures, any AI investment is a guess with an invoice attached.

**Three blocks**

> **Not every inefficiency is an AI opportunity.** A share of what slows your business requires AI. The remainder requires a process change, or better use of software you already license. Telling them apart requires examination, not a demonstration.

> **The cost of the problem is rarely quantified.** You know which processes create friction. You don't know what they cost you monthly, which leaves any investment decision unsupported.

> **There's no internal benchmark.** Your team understands the operation better than any outside party will. What they lack is a reference for what AI delivers in practice, what it costs, and what timeline is realistic.

**Closing line**
> We establish all three: where AI applies in your business, what the problem costs you now, and what the return would be.

## Section 3 — Where AI actually pays

**H2**
> AI returns show up in six places more often than anywhere else.

**Body**
> Some of these recover cost. Others recover revenue that was being lost without anyone recording it. These are where we look first.

**Six blocks**

> **Quote turnaround.** AI produces the quote from your own pricing the moment an enquiry arrives. Response time is frequently the deciding factor in whether the work is won.

> **After-hours response.** AI answers enquiries at any hour and escalates only what requires your judgment. No enquiry waits until morning because nobody was available.

> **Recurring enquiries.** AI answers the questions your team fields every week, in your own language, without consuming staff time that has higher-value uses.

> **Management reporting.** AI compiles your operating figures continuously, so reporting is available on demand rather than produced on request.

> **Document collection.** AI requests, tracks and follows up the documents your work depends on, and reports what remains outstanding.

> **Key-person dependency.** AI captures how specialized work is performed, which reduces the operational risk of one individual being unavailable.

**Closing line**
> One of these costs your business materially more than the others. We identify which, and establish what it's worth, before you commit anything.

**CTA**
> Book 20 minutes — find out which one is yours

## Section 4 — How we find it

**H2**
> Assessment first. Business case second. Build only if the case holds.

> **We map one process end to end.** Who handles it, at what frequency, and where it stalls. Delay accounts for most of the cost in most processes, and appears in nobody's budget.

> **We quantify the opportunity.** Built from your figures, with every assumption stated so you can test it. A weak business case is worth establishing before capital is committed.

> **We recommend the lowest-cost option that works.** Frequently that's software you already license, configured correctly. Sometimes it's a change to the sequence of work. Sometimes it's AI. Sometimes the recommendation is to do nothing.

> **We build and deploy.** A fixed fee, agreed in advance.

**Closing line**
> We deliver both the assessment and the implementation. When the business case doesn't hold, we tell you with the same confidence as when it does.

**Body**
