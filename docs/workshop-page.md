## What this page has to do

A visitor arrives here for one reason: they know AI matters, they have more than one candidate, and they cannot rank them. The page has to make choosing the workshop feel like the sensible move rather than an admission that they don't know their own business.

It also has to answer the question the free discovery call creates: **why pay for a workshop when the call costs nothing?** The answer is that they do different jobs. Discovery examines one problem already identified. The workshop is for when nothing has been identified yet, or when there are six candidates and no way to rank them.

## Section 2 — When a workshop is the right first step

**H2**
> This is for the list, not the single problem.

**Body**
> If you can already name the one thing you'd fix, you don't need a workshop. Say so on the call and we'll go straight to discovery. The workshop exists for the more common position: several candidates, no ranking, and no capacity to establish which one matters.

**Three blocks**

> **You have candidates, not a decision.** Quoting, onboarding, reporting, client questions, document handling — all plausible, none obviously first.

> **Nobody has measured what any of them cost.** Without that, ranking them is opinion. Every internal discussion ends where it started.

> **It never reaches the top of the list.** There's no deadline forcing it, so it loses to work that has one — every quarter, indefinitely.

## Section 3 — What happens on the day

**H2**
> A day with your team, on your workflows.

**Body**
> We spend the day with the people who actually do the work, not only the people who describe it. We follow real workflows end to end, establish where time and money go, and test each candidate against what AI can genuinely do today.

**Scope paragraph — do not cut**
> A day covers one area of a business, not all of it. Before booking, we agree which area and roughly how many workflows are in scope — client intake, month-end, quoting, whatever is costing you most. A larger operation, or several departments, needs more than a day, and we'll tell you that on the call rather than halfway through.

**Four blocks**

> **We map the work as it runs.** Who touches each process, how often, and where it waits. Delay accounts for most of the cost in most workflows and appears in nobody's budget.

> **We put figures against each candidate.** Built from your numbers, with every assumption stated openly so you can challenge it.

> **We test what AI actually does.** Some candidates are AI problems. Others need a process change or better use of software you already license. We separate them.

> **We rank them.** By what each is costing you now, against what it would take to fix.

**CTA**
> Book your free 20-minute call

**Body**
> The workshop ends with a written recommendation. It's yours to keep and yours to use — including with somebody else, if you'd rather.

**Four blocks**

> **What each would take.** Scope, and an indicative figure, so the shortlist is a decision rather than a wish list.

> **A recommendation on what to build first.** With the reasoning, so you can disagree with it on the evidence.

> **What we'd advise against.** Candidates that look attractive and aren't worth building, and why.

**Closing line**

## Section 6 — Before you book

**Build note:** mark up with `FAQPage` schema.

**Why would I pay for this when the call is free?**
> They do different jobs. The call, and the discovery that follows it, examines one problem you've already identified. The workshop is for when nothing has been identified yet, or when there are several candidates and no way to rank them. If you can name your one problem, skip the workshop — we'll tell you so on the call.

**What if the workshop concludes we shouldn't build anything?**
> Then that's the recommendation, in writing, with the reasoning. Knowing that a job isn't worth automating is worth more than discovering it after paying for a build.

**We're a larger business. Is one day enough?**
> Often not, and we'd rather say so before you book. One day covers one area properly. Covering several means either a longer engagement or picking the area most worth examining first — usually the second, because a ranked list of one area you can act on beats a survey of five you can't.

**Who needs to be in the room?**
> The people who perform the work, and someone who can make a decision. A day spent only with management produces a description of the process rather than the process.

**Do we have to implement with you?**
> No. The document is yours. The credit exists because we'd like to build it, not because you're committed to us.

**How long before we get the recommendation?**
> Shortly after the day, while the detail is still current. We agree the date when the workshop is booked.

## [CONFIRM] before this page ships

**The deliverable.** Section 4 states the document includes an indicative figure per candidate. That is the strongest reason to pay for a workshop rather than wait for free discovery — but only include it if that is genuinely what you produce.

**The credit window.** Section 5 states the credit with no expiry. An open-ended credit is a liability that never closes. If a window applies, it belongs in the proposal rather than on the page.

**Delivery timing.** Section 6 says the recommendation arrives "shortly after the day". Replace with the real commitment once you've decided it, or leave as agreed-per-engagement.

**Whether the price varies by scope.** Section 5 says the fee follows scope. Confirm that is true rather than a flat CAD $1,000 with "from" as protection.

**What counts as one area.** The page says a day covers one area and names client intake, month-end and quoting as examples. Agree internally what a defensible "area" is, because it's the boundary that protects the fee. Without it, a client can reasonably expect the day to cover everything they mention.
